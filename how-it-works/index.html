How it works

Four steps from handover to routine.

The first few weeks are the work. After that, management should be something you notice only when the monthly report arrives.

Step 01

Onboarding survey

We meet you at the vessel and walk it together. The purpose is to establish an honest baseline: what condition the boat is in today, what has been deferred, and what needs attention first.

You tell us how you use the boat — weekends in the harbour, long offshore runs, guests aboard, or laid up for part of the year. That usage pattern is what makes the maintenance schedule realistic rather than theoretical.

  • Walkthrough of the vessel with the owner
  • Current condition recorded and photographed
  • Deferred and outstanding items identified up front
  • Usage pattern and owner priorities captured

Step 02

Systems inventory

We record every major system aboard with its make, model, serial number, current hours, and warranty status. Engines, generator, watermaker, HVAC, electronics, hull and running gear.

This is the unglamorous step that makes everything afterwards work. A maintenance schedule is only as good as the equipment list behind it, and it is the thing most owners discover they have never had written down in one place.

  • Make, model, and serial captured per system
  • Engine and generator hours logged as the tracking baseline
  • Warranty windows recorded before they lapse unnoticed
  • The inventory belongs to you and travels with the vessel

Step 03

Maintenance schedule

With the inventory in place, each system gets its service intervals. Those intervals run on two clocks — calendar time and engine hours — and whichever comes first is what triggers the work.

Each item carries lead time ahead of its due date, so haul-outs, bottom jobs, zinc changes, oil services, and survey deadlines surface while there is still time to book a yard slot rather than after the window has closed.

  • Intervals assigned per system from the manufacturer's schedule
  • Dual tracking on calendar date and engine hours
  • Lead-time alerts before an item becomes due
  • Work orders raised, dispatched, and tracked to completion

Step 04

Monthly report

Each month you receive a written report: what was completed, what is scheduled next, the current hours and condition of your systems, and every cost incurred that period — with photographs of the work.

The intent is that you can read it in three minutes and know the full state of your vessel, and that if you ever want to check a claim about what was done, the record is already there.

  • Work completed, with photo evidence
  • Work scheduled for the coming period
  • Current engine hours and system condition
  • Itemised costs and management fee
