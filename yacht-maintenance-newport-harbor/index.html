Scheduled, not reactive

Two clocks, tracked at once.

Every system aboard has its own service rhythm. Some are driven by the calendar — sun and salt do not care whether the boat left the dock. Others are driven by engine hours, and a vessel used hard for three months needs attention a lightly used sistership does not.

We track both. Each system in your vessel's inventory carries its own interval, and work is generated with enough lead time to actually book a vendor and a yard slot.

The record

Every job leaves evidence.

A work order is not closed because a vendor said it was done. It is closed when the invoice is attached and the technician has uploaded photographs of the completed work.

This matters most at resale. A documented, continuous service history is the difference between a survey that goes smoothly and one that turns into a negotiation.

Common questions.

How do you know when something is due?

Each system in the vessel inventory carries an interval based on calendar time, engine hours, or both. Engine hours are logged on every visit and the schedule regenerates from the current reading, with lead time built in so a haul-out or a yard booking can actually be arranged.

What happens to the record of the work?

A work order is not closed until the vendor invoice is attached and photographs of the completed work are uploaded. That record is attached to the vessel rather than to a person’s memory, and it is visible to the owner as soon as it is filed.
